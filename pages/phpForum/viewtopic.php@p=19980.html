My take on the "car still runs after button is released" syndrome:
I've noticed the "syndrome" happens when my Super Bit goes out of range. It doesn't happen at all when in range, except when the batt is almost drained. Different motors didn't seem to affect the "syndrome" either.

I used to race 1/12 scale electric and I remember this happening to our cars. The servos just stay where they are (usually full throttle headed towards a curb! ) when the receiver looses signal, either by going out of range or the batts on the car dying.

I suspect something similar is happening on the Super Bits. That could explain Ahh5hit's problem: (really bad case of the "keepin' on going" after I let off. It is also hesitant about going at all after that). His car is just outside it's range and that's why it hesitates.

What do you guys think?

Otherwise, I love my Super Bits!
I'm running a Perfection 3.8. I can pop wheelies with it on a stock Super Bit chassis, imagine what a 4.2 can do!
I'll have to get a couple 4.2's and see for myself!
