Sorry for jumping in late here (been on vacation for the week).

Hue says they suck human puddin, I say they're a throwback to the 80's Penny Racers and if yur old enough to remember the Penny Racers (and maybe you even liked/collected them)...Then the DigiQ is a cool thing.

But automatically assuming all DigiQ's suck because you got 2 duds from some guy off eBay...?

I see them 2 kits being dead for 1 of 2 reasons; 1. They ain't actually brand new like the guy says in the auction and were used until the internal car battery died of use. Or 2. They were on the shelf, brand new, since the day they were made and the shelf life of the battery expired.

Considering the excellent result you got from the guy, I'd say #2 was what happened...And in that case I would suggest the same thing micro_man suggests; put the car through several charge cycles. If the car doesn't take a charge at all? Well, you could try a few rounds of putting it on/off the charger. If all else fails, you've got some interesting parts to play with.

Here's my suggestion about the Micro R/C cars:
- If you're old enough to have enjoyed the Penny Racers, check out the DigiQ. These things ARE fun if you've got extremely limited play space (like coffee table only)
- If you're just in the market for a micro sized R/C car with proportional control, a great choice is Radio Shack's ZipZaps SE cars (especially the newer generation kits)
- If you're looking for pure fun, tiny package, run basically anywhere R/C? Then the Radio Shack ZipZaps Monster Trucks is the way to go.
- Lastly, if it's more about the looks (but still plenty of fun to race around) The Bit-CharG (aka MicroSizers) is the way to go. The shells on Bits/MS cars are superior (in proportion and looks) to ZipZaps.

There you have it.

Hmmm, never heard about clearing the charging circuits in the controller before.

My understanding is that the battery IN THE CAR (1/4aaa size @ 80ma/h) has been sitting for so long, the voltage on them badboys are so low they won't take a proper charge.

If that's the case, then you've got to keep putting it on/off the charger; put the car on the charger for like a couple of minutes even if the charge light doesn't come on. Then take it off for a couple of minutes. Repeat until either you're tired from doing it or it finally starts charging.
