Build your own charger (and stop buying batteries)

  • Quote

Post by SuperFly » Fri Aug 15, 2003 1:23 am

I am finally getting around to posting this simple tutorial/how to. What we're going to do is make an AC charger out of commonly available parts with the least amount of hassle. My kind of mod! Since I didn't take pictures as I was building it, we're going to deconstruct it. Here's a picture of the final product:



You will need to make a trip to Walmart and Radio Shack. You will need a soldering iron, a little bit of wire, glue/epoxy, a charging pad from one of your many Bit Char-g or Microsizer controllers, a drill, and about an hour of time.

At Walmart, get a MotorWorks car, whichever you like, as long as it has the charging base. I like using the Motorworks base as a foundation mainly because it has a button to start the charge, so doing mulitple charges or longer charging time doesn't require you to change a capacitor or take the car on and off. Plus, it can operate on batteries if you're somewhere without electricity, and works for both single and dual cell cars. And it has a nifty stash box, er storage compartment on the bottom. Also available from Walmart, get this adapter:



Hue35 and I conducted a federally funded study over a 3 month period to determine the best adapter for charging your Bits. This one is the best. 1000mA is the rating you are looking for, though 800 will work (1500 would probably work too, but would take it's toll on your car batteries). It aslo does multiple voltages, so charging dual and triple cell cars only requires the flick of the switch. Be wary of Radio Shack adapters, they have build-in short circuit protection, which renders them useless for this purpose. Actually, one adapter we found that seemed to charge the cars better was a Sony 3 volt adapter that was rated at 700mA, but put out substantially more than that. Hue35 will have to add that detail, I forgot the meter rating. On the end of the cord you can see the N Adaptaplug you'll need to get at Radio Shack. It's kind of weird that Walmart and Radio Shack products work together.

At Radio Shack, get a power jack plug (M or N will do, mine's N I believe), and a corresponding Adaptaplug. The jack you need looks like this:



As you can see, I drilled another hole, and started a second (third time's a charm!) The original hole was when I had this wired up with a male Adaptaplug connector that I plugged straight in to the power adaptor. the second hole that I started is where I wanted to put the jack. The reason it has to be where it is has to do with the location of the PCB and battery compartment inside the charging base, and the room you will need for the back end of the jack.

Before we wire it up and put the jack in, we remove the MotorWorks charging pad and replace it with a Bit-Char-g pad like this:



As you can see, I shimmed the sides with some other plastic bits and doused it with model glue. The height is fairly critical, as in don't make it too low so your car's tires keep it from seating on the pad. I also reinforced mine on the back side, since this little piece of plastic is going to see lots of action. You can tell by how rounded off all the plastic is how many charges this base has given.

Once the glue has dried, solder the wires on to the PCB where the MotorWorks pad was attached originally. You have to keep the red/black wires going to the same places on the PCB, but it doesn't matter from the jack to the battery contacts. You can control polarity with the Adaptaplug by sticking it on the cable 180 degrees either way.

Heres the inside:



I left the wires from the battery compartment in place so I can still use this charging base with batteries. All you need to do is solder the tabs on the jack to the contacts where the battery leads go from the batteries to the PCB.

I even rigged up a little jumper so I can do both single and dual cell cars with batteries.



That's it! Boom, done.

I've done tons of mods over the last 8 months, and this is by no means the flashiest, but is by far the most useful. Charging your car with the same batteries you use to transmit signal creates two problems: inconsistent charges and reduced transmitting power (= less range). Since I started using this, I haven't changed the batteries in my controller, and I use it quite a bit. Hue35 and I each have one of these, and it makes our charges so consistent that if we have equally conditioned batteries, we will run out of charge within 20-30 seconds of eachother. We usually double or triple charge with no ill effects to our car's batteries. we have a couple cars with 200mA batteries, and those take a quadruple charge, no problem. The beauty of it is just hitting that button when the light goes out (and trying desperately to count to 3 without forgetting).

I may add more later, but that about covers it. Questions?

Post by SuperFly » Sun Feb 01, 2004 6:50 am

A couple things to check:

1. You have to take the batteries out in order to use the AC adaptor.

2. Is the voltage, polarity and mAh of the adaptor correct? It has to be 3V for single cell and/or 4.5V for dual cells. mAh should be between 500 and 1000. Polarity is critical. There should be a little diagram on the adaptor that shows the positive and negative arrangement of the jack coming off the adaptor, make sure you have wired the socket accordingly. If your adaptor has a hard-wired male end ( ), just switch the wires from the female socket to the circuit board.

3. The problem I describe in the tutorial with Radio Shack adaptors has the same symtom. I think it has something to do with short-circuit protection. Someone corrrect me if I'm wrong, but charging a battery with an adaptor is more or less shorting it out. The RS adaptors don't work for this reason, maybe Philips has the same issue. Try a different adaptor if you can.

Hope this helps.
