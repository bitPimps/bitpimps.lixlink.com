The front end is only gonna drop when you jump if you let off the throttle. Usually a 4wd vehicle is bit nose heavy, so you just stay on the throttle.

I'm not sure what you're so nervous about jumping for, I never really considered it a skill.


Have you had any experience with any hobby grade RCs larger than Mini-Zs or 1/18th scale? These cars are really much more durable than I believe you think they are. I used to just sit and pop 7ft air with my Stampede over and over again. It would only strip the cheap plastic outdrive cup after about 10-15 runs of that. Sure I could've got the steel ones for $10, but I just kept buying the plastic ones for $3, kept spares in my pit box. I used to break steering knuckles, but only because I would nail curbs at top speed because I was too lazy to walk 30ft to put it on the sidewalk, and it looked cool as hell. The guy I first sold it to, decided to play demolition derby with the neighbor kid's Tonka truck. Of course the Tonka truck won. I bought it back, and only had to replace the steering knuckles, and servo.

I mean come on, demolition derby with a Tonka truck? This shit is tough, and I'm sure a high end 1/8th scale nitro buggy is gonna be tougher than my Stampede was, but that's what I was telling you about cheap weak parts being designed in.

You mention the chassis flex in the Ofna, and that's exactly the kind of thing I was trying to tell you about. A little chassis flex not only makes it an easier more forgiving car to drive, but allows things to flex, rather than break. If you've never driven this type of car before, and you're worried about just taking a jump, I'm not seeing how you're gonna be driving at a pro level right off the bat, so you shouldn't worry about having the pro set up. I'm trying to tell you this because I spend hundreds of dollars to learn the hard way.

Honestly I'm not sure why I keep coming back to this thread.

Thats not good, it would eat gears like it was going out of style, all from the stock puny weak engine. Plus the diffs exploded too. Thats what I don't want to get into, even when the MT2 was hopped up it was still not that great.

So far I've only driven 18th scale, I have an 18T with Mamba 8000 and it's a hoot in the alleys, but sucks out in the dirt unless it's prepared. Either my jump isn't the greatest, or I really am that bad. I either nose dived or landed too rear heavy. I couldn't quite control it mid-air yet, it would nose dive, so I'd get on the gas a bit, either it made it flip onto the rear bumper, or didn't work. I didn't practice a whole lot, so thats one reason, but I couldn't level it off if I wanted to.

I just don't want to dish out money for crashes, thats all. Being 16, going to school and can only manage to work only one day week doesn't necessarily bring a whole heck of a lot of dough, plus I don't sell dope like many other teenagers in my school, but I don't drink, so that saves me money (not because I don't want to, because I'm not popular enough to know where all the parties are until after they are done, plus I don't see the point of getting drunk).
