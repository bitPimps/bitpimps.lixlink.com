Post by crazydave » Tue Jun 08, 2004 9:51 am

I'll tell you what, all that PCB is doing timing the charge. I prefer to charge longer anyways, so I just wire the batteries straight to the charging terminals.

Remember, if you do this don't leave you're car unattended, excessive charging may cause a fire. Don't forget the ground goes to the drive side of the car, and also I think 4.5v is a bit much for single cells , I'd pull out one of the batteries, and wire it with 2 cells.

Also I use a volt meter to watch for voltage drop while charging. After you do this for a while you get a knack for when to pull it off.

That just my way of going about things, but it's worked out extremely well over the last year or so.

I also wonder about that whole PCB thing, because it seems to work fine charging on all my other shit, so I figure it's fine.
Yeah, if it's just this one car, and all your others work fine, then it probably aint the PCB, and the direct wiring thing aint gonna help.

Check and make sure the terminal in the car is pushed all the way to the back. Sometimes the get pushed a little forwards, and the charger terminal slip behind it.
Anyhow, I'll be trying out that whole 45sec direct charge. Personally, I'd like to adapt the whole charger for 4.5v DC operation. What milliamps and other specs does anyone here think would be closest to the output of three AAA batteries in that charger?
You want something more or less around 800mA. Remember, the current from the wall varies, so with manufacturing variences, the current is not gonna be exactly the rated #. If you use less mA you'll have to charge longer, if you use more, you'll have shorter charging times. For single cells you want 3v. If you get an adapter with switchable voltages, you can use it for your multi cell cars later on.

Also, What is a thunder charger and where can I get one for really cheap?
Thunder Chargers were $5 at BigLots. Most BigLots have sold out of them, but there still a few out there.
