New Zip Zap Wheelset

  • Quote

Post by crazydave » Sun Jul 10, 2005 2:20 pm

I was at the Shack picking up some crap, when I came across these.


They looked pretty cool, and for $4.99 I figured what the heck, I could use some more fatties. Here's a close up in the sun, to show off the bling factor.



I was surprised to find there's a few extra tires included. A full set of 4 of each fattie, plus 8, instead of 6 fattie adapters. Here's break down as described on the box.




I was also suprised to find them experimenting with compound and shape.

Here's the Hard Slicks. They are not slick, but they sure are hard. Rock hard, think drifting tires. Note the inside edge has no lip, so it's easier to slip on.


Here's the off road tires. They seem to be of a medium compound, similar to like Super Slick tires. Notice they have a extra thick sidewall,, but just on one side.


At first I thought the larger sidewall would look cool on the muscle cars, but then I realized, because it's only one side, it's gonna be uneven, and it was.


Nice try, but not quite on that one. Luckily the Precision tires are good, so all is no bust. They seem to be of a slightly softer sticker compound, have a nice flat profile, even on the wheels, and a have a cool tread pattern that looks like some kind of high speed V-rated thing. I like these tires.


Just to check out the look, I stuck them on my vette. They don't look bad, but ultimatly, I didn't think they work with my vette. The 5-spoke ones would probably look nice with any of the truck bodies though.

Chrome plated wheels have always been a tight fit. Since all these wheels are chrome plated, you'll have to take an excato blade, and scrape the plating off the edge where the wheel goes into the hub. Be careful not to take plating off in the front, where it can be seen.

Also pay attention to the little nubs where they came off the molding trees, I had to trim a couple of those off to.
